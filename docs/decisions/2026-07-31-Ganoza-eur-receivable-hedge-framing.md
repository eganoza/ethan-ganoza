## Executive Summary (≤150 words)

We expect to receive €4,500,000 in one year (July 31, 2027). Although the euro amount is fixed, its USD value is not. At a planning rate of EUR/USD 1.10, the receivable supports a $4,950,000 cash-flow expectation. If the euro weakens to 1.00 before settlement, proceeds fall to $4,500,000—a $450,000 shortfall that could impair budgeted margins, liquidity, or investment capacity. I recommend approval to evaluate a hedge now, before the exposure becomes a realized USD result. The objective is not to predict EUR/USD; it is to select an appropriate balance among cash-flow certainty, participation in favorable euro movements, and hedging cost.

## Background & Objectives

This is a foreign-currency receivable: our firm will receive euros but reports and plans in U.S. dollars. A fall in EUR/USD is adverse because each euro converts into fewer dollars. The business consequence is a volatile USD cash inflow despite a fixed €4.5 million sale. The primary objective is to establish a defensible hedge decision and its USD cash-flow implications before settlement. A secondary objective is to preserve transparency: management should understand what certainty is purchased, what upside is surrendered, and what cost or balance-sheet capacity is used.

## Methods

The analysis will compare three hedge families. A forward contract would lock the rate at which we sell the euros on July 31, 2027, providing complete budget certainty; the trade-off is forfeiting upside if the euro appreciates, plus counterparty and settlement mechanics. A money-market hedge would borrow euros today, convert at spot, and invest the dollars, replicating forward-like certainty through interest rates. It is useful if forward pricing or availability is unfavorable, but consumes borrowing capacity and requires more transactions. A EUR put option would set a minimum USD conversion rate while retaining upside if the euro strengthens. Its flexibility is valuable, but the premium is paid whether or not the protection is needed.

## Limitations & Next Steps

This memo is framing, not a hedge recommendation: rates, interest differentials, option premiums, and credit terms remain to be measured.
